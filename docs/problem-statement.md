# Problem Statement

## Who is affected?
Agile software development teams, Scrum Masters, Product Owners, and project clients or stakeholders

## What exactly is going wrong?
Traditional forecasting relies on simple deterministic averages—like mean velocity—which falsely assumes a perfectly smooth, predictable development process and ignores real-world bottlenecks, delays, and variance.

## Why does it matter?
This causes teams to fall into the "Flaw of Averages," leading to over-promised deliverables, frequently missed deadlines, and an inability to objectively calculate or communicate schedule risks to clients.

## Problem statement (3 to 5 sentences)
Agile software development teams and their stakeholders frequently struggle with inaccurate release forecasting because they rely on simple deterministic baselines, such as dividing remaining work by the team's historical average velocity. This method falsely assumes a perfectly smooth process and ignores real-world bottlenecks, leading to the "Flaw of Averages" where promised delivery dates have a very low probability of actual success. Consequently, projects are frequently delayed, and teams lack a mathematical way to explain schedule uncertainty or true project risk to their clients.
